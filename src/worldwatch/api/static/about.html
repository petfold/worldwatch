About Worldwatch

What this is, what it measures, and how to opt out.

What this is

Worldwatch is a non-commercial, open-source research project (github.com/petfold/worldwatch). It watches free, open data streams — earthquakes, weather warnings, radiation monitoring, internet reachability, markets, news — learns what is normal for each, and flags calibrated departures from normal, aiming to notice unusual events early.

Almost everything it does is passive: it reads public feeds and APIs, with conditional requests where supported, within their published rate limits, and with a descriptive User-Agent:
worldwatch/0.1 (global anomaly monitor; https://github.com/petfold/worldwatch; contact via repo)

Active measurements (internet reachability)

To see internet outages quickly and evenly around the world, Worldwatch also sends a small number of standard, harmless probes from one server:

From158.220.117.131 (categor.io), a single server in Germany
NTP serversOne standard NTP client request (48 bytes, UDP port 123) every 2 minutes to servers listed in the NTP Pool country zones — within the pool's guidance of at most one query per 64 seconds per server. We read only whether a reply arrived and how long it took; the time value itself is unused.
RIPE Atlas anchorsA TCP connection to port 80, closed immediately without sending data, every 2 minutes, to RIPE Atlas anchors — hosts deployed to be measured.
ScaleAbout 8 NTP servers and 4 anchors per country, spread across different networks; roughly 800 hosts in total, about 150 KB of traffic per round.
What is keptReachable or not, and round-trip time. Only per-country counts ("5 of 12 unreachable") are ever shown; individual target addresses are never published.

Opting out

If you operate one of these hosts and would rather not be probed, open an issue at github.com/petfold/worldwatch/issues with the address or range. It will be added to the exclusion list and no longer probed, typically within a day. Questions about any other traffic from this server are welcome the same way.

Caveats

This is an experiment. Its signals are statistical ("unusual for this place and time"), not verified reports, and much of the world is covered thinly — the map shows where our own internet probing is thin or absent. Do not use it for safety decisions; follow your local authorities.
